## Arth (meaning) sources

As cited by the Shabad OS Database:

- Singh, Sahib (Prof.). *Sri Guru Granth Darpan*. Vol. 1–10, Raaj Publishers, Hoshiarpur/Jalandhar, 1972.
- Jaggi, Ratan Singh. *Sri Dasam Granth*. Gobind Sadan, New Delhi, 2007.
- Singh, Vir (Bhai). *Varan Bhai Gurdas Steek*. 22nd ed., Bhai Vir Singh Sahit Sadan, New Delhi, 2012.
- Singh, Sampuran. *Kabit Sawayan Bhai Gurdas Ji Steek*. 2012.
- Singh, Ganda. *Bhai Nand Lal Granthavali*. 4th ed., Punjabi University, Patiala, 2009.
- Singh, Manmohan. *Sri Guru Granth Sahib (English & Punjabi Translation)*. SGPC, Amritsar, 2006–2009 (used where the Darpan has no entry).

The "Hindi script" meaning option shows the Punjabi arth written in Devanagari. It is not a Hindi translation.

## English translation sources

As cited by the Shabad OS Database:

- Khalsa, Sant Singh (Dr.). *Siri Guru Granth Sahib Ji*. 2013.
- Kohli, Surinder Singh (Dr.). *Sri Dasam Granth Sahib Translation*. Sikh National Heritage Trust, 2003.
- Singh, Jodh. *Vārān Bhāī Gurdās: Text, Transliteration, and Translation*. 2nd ed., B. Chattar Singh Jiwan Singh, Amritsar, 2013.
- Puri, Shamsher Singh. *Kabitt Swayye Bhai Gurdas Ji*. 1st ed., Singh Brothers, Amritsar, 2007.
- Bindra, Pritpal Singh (trans.). *Kalaam-e-Goya*. Institute of Sikh Studies, Chandigarh, 2003.

English is bundled for every line of Sri Guru Granth Sahib, about half of Sri Dasam Granth, and the writings of Bhai Gurdas and Bhai Nand Lal.
